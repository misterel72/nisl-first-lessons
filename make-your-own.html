Build it yourself

Make your own with Gemini Canvas

You do not need to be a coder. Start with a clear classroom problem, ask Gemini Canvas to build a small app, preview it, test it, and then improve it through conversation.

Open Gemini Back to First Lessons
1

Start in Canvas

Go to Gemini. Choose Add files → Canvas, then tell Gemini what you want to build. Ask for an interactive web app, not a document or presentation.

2

Describe the classroom problem

Good vibe coding starts with the job, not the technology. Explain who will use it, what they need to do, the age of the pupils and what a successful result should feel like.

3

Copy this starter prompt

Create an interactive web app for teachers to use with students. PURPOSE: I want to create a classroom resource that helps with: [DESCRIBE THE PROBLEM OR ACTIVITY]. AUDIENCE: Students are aged [AGE / YEAR GROUP]. The teacher should be able to use it immediately on a classroom screen. REQUIREMENTS: - Make it simple, attractive and easy to understand from across the room. - Use large readable text and clear buttons. - Do not require student logins. - Do not collect personal data. - Keep instructions short and student-friendly. - Include all prompts, questions, examples, statements or scenarios needed to run the activity. Do not leave the teacher to invent missing content. - Include a reset or new-round button where useful. - Make it work well on a laptop and classroom display. - Use British English. - Keep the design calm, modern and school appropriate. - Build the working app in Gemini Canvas and show me a preview. PEDAGOGY: The activity should encourage participation, thinking and discussion rather than passive clicking. Where appropriate, include a short reflection at the end. Before finishing, test the flow as if you were a teacher using it live with 25 students.
4

Preview it like a teacher

Use Canvas Preview. Click every button. Read it from a distance. Ask yourself: “Could I teach this tomorrow without inventing anything?” If the answer is no, keep refining.

Useful test: If the app says “ask some questions”, “use a few examples” or “repeat with more statements”, make Gemini supply the actual questions, examples or statements.
5

Refine by talking to Gemini

You do not need to rewrite the code. Tell Gemini what is wrong in ordinary language. Be specific about the classroom experience you want.

Try:
“The instructions are too wordy. Make them readable from the back of the room.”

“Add 15 ready-to-use questions so the teacher does not need to invent them.”

“Make this suitable for Year 4 without making it childish.”

“Add a student view with no teacher notes.”
6

The quality check

Before you use it with pupils, run this final prompt:

Act as an experienced classroom teacher and quality-assure this app before I use it with students. Check: 1. Is every instruction clear enough to use without explanation? 2. Does the teacher have every prompt, question, example, statement and scenario needed? 3. Is anything likely to embarrass, exclude or pressure a student into personal disclosure? 4. Is the reading level appropriate for the age group? 5. Could the activity work with 25–30 students? 6. Are there any dead ends, confusing buttons or missing reset options? 7. Does the activity produce useful learning, discussion or insight rather than just novelty? Fix any problems you find and show me the improved version.
Keep it school-safe: avoid collecting names, emails, photographs or other personal information unless there is a clear approved school need. Test anything AI-generated before putting it in front of pupils.

Canvas, not Canva

For this kind of interactive resource, use Gemini Canvas. The separate Canva connected app in Gemini is designed for creating and editing Canva designs. Google currently states that the Canva connected app requires a personal Google Account and is English-only, so it is not the route we recommend for this staff workflow.
